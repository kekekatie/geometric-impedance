# The 2D onset-lip bracket: thin window + local bracket vs thick window everywhere

*Design and pre-registration for the final experimental pillar, written **before**
any 2D code exists. Consolidates the 1D thread (`b43826a` ... `4e439aa`) into one 2D
surface-code shot. Companion figure: `onset_lip_2d.svg` (generated by
`make_figure_2d.py`).*

## 0. Why this experiment

The 1D chain gave us, in order: a witness (streaming decoding sheds information a
delayed loss report needs to re-judge a committed decision); the restoration (the
shed "reason" is **exactly** a small local bracket of detectors around the lost
qubit, at the onset rounds); the deadline (that bracket must be kept *live*, not
reconstructed from a future that has not happened); and the abstention result (the
cheapest reliable flag for "I threw away something that matters" is sensitivity to
the discarded bracket, not generic low confidence).

Every piece of that points at one concrete decoder-engineering claim, which only 2D
can make interesting:

> **You do not need a thick sliding window everywhere. You need a thin window
> everywhere plus a tiny *local* witness bracket kept live around each lost qubit.**

In 1D a "local bracket" is almost the whole chain, so the saving is invisible. In 2D
the bracket is a fixed O(1) diamond around the loss while a thick window costs the
whole patch area times the report latency — so "thin + local bracket" vs "thick
everywhere" is a real, measurable trade, and it is the natural capstone.

## 1. The picture first (what the bracket looks like in 2D)

### 1a. Space: a lost qubit sits in a diamond of four checks

Rotated surface code, checkerboard of plaquettes. Data qubits sit at plaquette
corners; each **bulk** data qubit is shared by four stabilizers — two X-type and two
Z-type — arranged as a diamond around it:

```
         (Z)                    legend
        /   \                   D   = lost data qubit
     (X)  D  (X)                (X) = X-plaquette check (detects Z errors)
        \   /                   (Z) = Z-plaquette check (detects X errors)
         (Z)                    the four checks touching D = its "star"
```

A lost atom is an **erasure**: it destroys both X and Z information, so it lights up
**all four** surrounding checks, not two. That four-check diamond — the loss site's
*star* — is the spatial witness bracket. In 1D the data qubit sat between **two**
checks; in 2D it sits inside a **four-check** neighbourhood. That is the whole
geometric step, and it is why the 2D bracket is a genuinely 2D object (a ring around
a point) rather than two endpoints.

At a **boundary** data qubit the star has three checks (edge) or two (corner), so the
bracket shrinks there — a prediction we test (D4).

### 1b. Spacetime: the bracket is that diamond, at the onset lip

Add time (rounds) as the vertical axis. A loss with onset round `s` opens a vertical
**tube** at the qubit's location, from `s` upward (the qubit is unknown thereafter).
The erasure's detectors are the diamond of four checks firing at the **floor** of the
tube (the onset round) and again at its resolution. Because the onset is ambiguous
(`s ∈ {s_a, s_b}`, the two plausible rounds the report cannot distinguish), the floor
is a short stack of two diamonds:

```
  time ↑        . . . . . . . . . .   <- resolution ring (top of the tube)
                        | tube |
     s_b  ───►   (Z)(X) D (X)(Z)      <- diamond at the later candidate onset
     s_a  ───►   (Z)(X) D (X)(Z)      <- diamond at the earlier onset = the LIP
                ─────────────────     <- commit cut: everything below is pruned
   committed    (pruned to frame + boundary)
     slab
```

The **lip** is the earliest-onset diamond — exactly as in 1D, where keeping only the
later-round detectors failed because the un-absorbable syndrome, the part that fixes
the committed logical's fate, lives at the earliest onset floor. The full 2D witness
bracket is therefore:

> **bracket(loss) = { the ≤4 stabilizers in the lost qubit's star } × { the onset
> interval rounds }**  — at most `4 × |onset interval|` detectors (≤ 8 for a bulk
> qubit, a 2-round interval), independent of the code distance `d`.

This is the object the thin window must keep live locally.

## 2. The two decoders under test

Same 2D patch, same noise + one delayed, site-labelled loss, same logical decision
rule. All decoders decide at a frozen deadline (the report round); none sees the
post-deadline future (scored against a same-deadline unpruned reference, as in 1D).

- **THICK-EVERYWHERE.** One global sliding window whose buffer depth `W_thick ≥ L`,
  the loss-report latency, *everywhere*. It never commits a slab until the report
  interval for every qubit in it has closed, so it always holds the full local
  history and re-judges optimally. Retained live state ≈ **O(d² · W_thick)** detectors
  (whole patch × thick buffer).

- **THIN + LOCAL BRACKET.** A thin global window, buffer `b_thin ≪ L` (just enough for
  ordinary decoding), **plus** a rule: when a loss is flagged at site `p`, keep the
  local bracket(p) — its star × onset interval — live past the cut, carried with the
  Pauli frame as a small side record. Retained live state ≈ **O(d² · b_thin) +
  n_loss · O(1)**. For sparse losses this is ~`W_thick / b_thin` times smaller.

- **THIN-ALONE** (control). The thin window with no bracket: it prunes the loss site
  like everything else. This is the 2D witness decoder — it should fail to re-judge.

- **SAME-DEADLINE REFERENCE** (scoring only). Unpruned full history, decided at the
  deadline. The gold standard for re-judgment, as in 1D.

## 3. Frozen protocol

- **Code.** Rotated surface code, distance `d = 3` (primary; 9 data, 8 stabilizers)
  and `d = 5` (secondary, for a clean bulk star), `T` rounds of stabilizer
  measurement, perfect prep/readout, standard circuit-level or phenomenological noise
  (frozen `p` for data and measurement). One logical observable, one convention.
- **Loss.** Exactly one data qubit erased (both X and Z), at a **bulk** site for the
  primary run (a boundary site for D4), onset `s` uniform over a frozen interval
  `O = {s_a, s_b}`, reported at a frozen delayed round after the commit cut. The
  report gives site + interval, not the true onset — the three timescales kept
  distinct, as in 1D. Onset models `ONSET_AVG` (primary) and `FREE_BOTH`, as 1D.
- **Decoders.** The four above. The thin window's commit/prune/boundary scheme is the
  2D generalization of the 1D sliding window (commit temporal slabs, carry the
  committed correction's boundary defects on the cut + a Pauli frame); documented and
  serialized so a fresh decoder resumes from the snapshot alone.
- **Bracket rule.** `bracket(p)` = the stabilizers in `p`'s star (≤4 bulk, 3 edge, 2
  corner) × the onset interval rounds. Retained live, keyed only by permitted
  observables.
- **Evaluation.** A bounded/weighted ensemble of allowed histories (one loss + up to
  `w_max` extra faults), documented sampling rule, plus a witness stress set of
  histories that collide in the thin decoder's serialized state. Scored at matched
  coverage where abstention is used.
- **Metrics (reported separately).** (i) re-judgment agreement with the same-deadline
  reference; (ii) logical error vs simulator truth; (iii) **retained live-state size**
  (detector count / bytes) for each decoder — the efficiency axis that makes this 2D
  experiment worth running. Tie handling by exact expectation (`stage1_6_tie_fix`).

## 4. Predictions (fixed now)

- **D1 — thin + bracket ≈ thick, far cheaper.** THIN+BRACKET matches THICK-EVERYWHERE
  on re-judgment agreement with the reference to within a small ε (target: both ≥ 0.99
  on the bulk-loss primary run), while retaining **O(W_thick / b_thin)** less live
  state. Conf ~70%.
- **D2 — the 2D witness persists.** THIN-ALONE has strictly higher re-judgment error
  than both (it sheds the bracket). Conf ~80%.
- **D3 — the diamond is minimal.** Dropping any single stabilizer from the star, or
  the lip (earliest-onset) rounds, degrades THIN+BRACKET's re-judgment — the 2D analog
  of the 1D "all four onset-bracket bits are needed." Conf ~55%.
- **D4 — the bracket shrinks at the boundary.** A boundary-adjacent loss is re-judged
  by a 3- or 2-stabilizer bracket; the retained-state saving is correspondingly
  larger near the edges. Conf ~60%.
- **D5 — frame redundancy carries over.** Given the full star × onset bracket, the
  committed frame bit is redundant for re-judgment (as in 1D, L6), but a strict subset
  of the bracket cannot be reconstructed from the frame. Conf ~55%.

## 5. What a positive result would mean

A clean D1 + D2 would establish the capstone in 2D: the information a streaming surface-
code decoder must keep live to re-judge a delayed loss is a **local, O(1), distance-
independent 2D bracket** around the lost qubit — so the robust design is a thin window
everywhere with a local witness bracket at each loss, not a thick window everywhere.
That is the paper's "the skin outlives the archive" rendered as a concrete,
quantified decoder-memory trade: keep the living skin thin, and keep one small scar-
record exactly where a tile went missing.

## 6. Scope and limits (known now)

- Small distances (`d = 3, 5`), one loss, one error-type-pair via full erasure, bounded
  fault weight, exact-Bayesian reference — a feasibility pillar, not a threshold or
  scaling claim.
- "Thin vs thick" is measured as retained live detector count, not wall-clock; the
  constant factors of a real decoder are out of scope.
- The 1D caveats carry: the reference is an optimal soft decoder, scored separately
  from truth; the witness stress set is selection-biased and reported as such.
- This file is the design; no 2D outcome has been computed. Implementation
  (`stage2_surface.py`) and results are a separate, later commit, following the repo's
  pre-registration-before-code discipline.
