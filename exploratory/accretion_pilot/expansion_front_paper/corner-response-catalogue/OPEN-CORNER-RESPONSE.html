What this says, in plain words

When the growing edge faces a tie, it mostly makes up its mind through a single tile meeting at a single corner — a keystone handshake — and it really is the corner doing it, not just any neighbour crowding in. But the same move does not always do the same thing: a good share of recurring moves settle only some of the time. Reality here has give.

This is a study of how a growing Penrose tiling settles its own open choices. It makes no claim about matter, time, or physical space. Whether the corner-handshake is the acceptance-window truncation of the directional-balance paper is Experiment B; whether stiffness predicts how long structures last is the bridge to the endurance work. Both are later rungs.
