# Exploratory choice tracking

Extend the two existing B continuations to slice 60 using their original streams and patient scheduler. Stop and report a jam if one occurs. Verify slices 1–20 against the existing cache.

Before every slice, inventory every frontier edge's candidate identities and count, including forced and zero-candidate sites. Record the selected edge whenever the scheduler guesses. Identify sites by undirected endpoint coordinates, not ordering in the frontier list. Record all earlier observations of a selected edge with multiple alternatives. Inventory starts before slice 1, not at the origin of the world.

Compare selected sites' availability and option sets between histories. Such comparison provides evidence of dependence on diverging histories, not a complete causal ancestry graph. Subsequent random draws and scheduling can also differ.

Render full patches and fixed contact close-ups from slice 11 through the stopping horizon. Open circles refer to the pre-slice state; coloured construction refers to additions within the slice. Do not draw causal arrows. The original scheduler chooses the frontier edge closest to the coordinate origin (with coordinate tie-breaks) only after no forced site remains. This priority is a possible source of apparent sequence.

Multiple candidate-bearing edges are not necessarily independent degrees of freedom: alternatives can overlap. Local admissibility does not prove unlimited extendibility. Growth slices do not represent calibrated physical time. This is an exploratory two-history test; no significance claim or physical object identification is planned.
