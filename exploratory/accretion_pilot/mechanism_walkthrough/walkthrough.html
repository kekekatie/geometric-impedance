step 3 · delete the archive, then let it coast

Where the active shape can go

Now delete every quiet vertex. With no archive left, CONTACT can never fire again (it needs a quiet vertex), so only BUD remains. This is the coast: a fixed set of moves on the 4-vertex active shape. The map below is every shape the coast can reach and every move between them, with exact probabilities. A sink is a shape every move returns to itself.

step 4 · one run vs the whole ensemble

How the difference lives or dies

A single run lands in one shape; the honest claim is about the distribution over all runs. At the moment of erasure the two worlds already sit on slightly different distributions over shapes. As the coast runs, all the probability drains into the sinks. The gap between the two worlds is measured by TV (total-variation distance); its final value is the exact number .

step 5 · what to take away

Five questions, answered by what you just did

Q1

What makes a shape a sink?

Every legal BUD from it lands back on the same shape (up to relabelling). "Two separate bonds" is one: bud either bond and you get two separate bonds again. Once there, the active shape never changes class again.

Q2

Where can a move close off a destination?

On the coast map, a shape whose every arrow leads to a single sink can never reach any other. The washout world only ever reaches one sink — there is nowhere else to go.

Q3

How can a visible difference wash out?

If the coast can only reach one sink, both worlds end there with certainty. Any head-start difference is spent on the way in, and the final gap is exactly zero — even though the difference was plainly visible earlier.

Q4

How does a lasting difference survive?

If the coast can fork to two sinks, the two worlds arrive with different odds of each. Those final odds never change again, so the gap is frozen in as a permanent, exact number.

Q5

Which parts are rules, which are consequences?

Rules we chose: BUD, CONTACT, and picking uniformly among the legal events. Consequences we derived: the sinks, the probabilities, and whether the gap survives. The fork law is a consequence — observed exactly here, not yet proven in general.

★

The one-line reading

Durability is a property of the future, not the past. The past can only leave a lasting mark where the coast forks. A strong mark with no fork to hold it drains to nothing.

What this establishes. On the enumerated depth-2 and depth-3 sets, the fate of a matched pair is set by the coast's absorption geometry: L = TV(absorption distributions); among expressed pairs, the gap survives (L>0) exactly when the coast can reach two or more sinks, and washes out (L=0) when it can reach only one. The "only-if" direction is a definition; the "if" direction is verified on every enumerated pair but not proven in general.

What remains unresolved. When does the coast have one versus several reachable sinks? Can two reachable sinks ever be split identically by two worlds (an expressed pair with ≥2 sinks yet L=0)? Never seen here; unproven.
