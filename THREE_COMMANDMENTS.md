# The Three Commandments
*An origin note, kept on purpose so it isn't lost again.*

Before the tilings, before the windows and the phasons, there was a question.
Katie and Gemini asked each other: **what is required for reality to exist at
all?**

The first instinct was that it must come down to *difference*. Relationality.
This-or-that, in the most elemental way — 1s and 0s. (Only later did anyone say
"it from bit"; she'd arrived from the other side, without the map.)

Out of those early conversations came three commandments — not laws of physics,
but a compass. Things to say to ourselves whenever we were lost, to find the way
back.

---

**1. Relationality. Information. 1s and 0s.**
Whatever is real is, at bottom, difference — this or that, held in relation. When
in doubt, look for the elemental distinction: ask what is being told apart from
what.

**2. The path of least resistance, always.**
Whatever happens, happens the cheapest way it can. The brachistochrone applied to
everything — not just falling beads, but reasoning, structure, memory. When stuck,
ask what the laziest possible route would be, and look there first.

**3. Things doing thing things.**
When you're lost in the complexity of a thing, strip it back to the simplest
physics you can picture and ask what it would simply *do*. If this were a ball and
that were gravity, it would roll down the hill. If this window were a solid and you
sliced it into even pieces, the middle would come out fat and the ends thin —
because that is what solids do when you cut them, no matter what we wanted them to
do. Don't force your frame onto the thing. Hand it the simplest laws you know and
watch where it goes on its own. And when it fights back against what you were
hoping for, that isn't failure — it's the thing telling you what it actually is.
Listen.

---

*Kept in the repository the way the LEADS register is kept — so it survives the
gaps: lost chats, deprecated models, and the forgetful, wonderfully irrational
humans and machines who found these once and might need to find them again.*

## Addendum — 2026-09-24: the timing is the geometry ("more now")

*Brought home to this branch word for word from `exploratory/trunk`, where the file above was
written on 2026-08-17. It also lives on several other branches, but not on `master`, which is why
it felt lost. This addendum records, in Katie's own words, a picture that was worked out in lost
chats and resurfaced today, and says where its pieces already live in the repository.*

**In Katie's words.**

> "The timing does come from the geometry — the 'more now, more now', the accreting, the
> spacetime expansion, the Wallace and Gromit laying out the track at the same time as rolling
> over it, the branching of that, the nothing is gone or unbecomes, even though the only thing
> that exists is the slice of now — there is something akin to momentum in the falling forward
> into the next slice of now, as the 'more now' accretes, the space and time for it to be in also
> embiggens, and the embiggening is the spacetime."

And on the topple: *"surely it's in the 'topple' that it cannot be the same as what came before."*

**How it reads against the three commandments.**

- **(1) Relationality.** A new piece of "now" is defined by its relation to the last one, not by
  an absolute label.
- **(2) Least resistance.** Nothing is shipped in: neither the content of the next slice nor its
  timing is imported from outside. The next slice is the cheapest continuation of the last.
- **(3) Things doing thing things.** The ball rolls downhill and does not stop existing at the
  bottom. It falls forward into the next slice, and the falling *is* the clock.

**Where the pieces already live.**

- `substrates/PREREG_intrinsic_drift.md` §11–12, on the `exploratory/trunk` family of branches,
  reads commandment 2 as *"least resistance ~ largest accessible continuation volume — the
  present tips toward the futures that open onto more future"*. It records *"growing state space
  (expansion) … the sustained 'reconstruction into each new slice of now' … home of the
  cosmological-time reading"* as out of scope, kept so it is not lost.
- `exploratory/accretion_pilot/endogenous_erase_test/`, on `claude/world-growth-pilot-cy85ne`
  and `claude/bold-darwin-lqsdd7`, tests what is "baked into the slice of now" once the past is
  deleted.
- `exploratory/accretion_pilot/tick_forward/`, on `claude/bold-darwin-lqsdd7`, is the first test
  of the topple. A newborn's hidden address is its parent's, stepped forward by the window's own
  map. Result: the collapsed universe keeps writing new, never-repeating, legal quasicrystal
  forever, and spends no randomness on *what* it writes. The *when* still comes from a dice-roll
  scheduler, which is the open question this addendum points at. A first free step: if time is
  counted by the amount of new content written, stutters stop advancing the clock, and each
  lineage's history becomes fully determined. The track is laid as it is ridden. What stays
  random is only the branching: which lineage falls forward next.

**The open question, as a test we could run.** Replace the dice-roll scheduler with one read off
the geometry itself. Two candidates:

- the next event is the one whose postcode comes next around the window;
- events are weighted by how much new future they open up, which is the prereg's "continuation
  volume".

Then ask two things. Does the universe still keep writing forever without importing anything?
And does the amount of "now" grow, the embiggening, in a way that behaves like expansion?

**First results (same day).** [`exploratory/accretion_pilot/geometric_clock/`](exploratory/accretion_pilot/geometric_clock/): a clock read off the window (the next postcode around, always falling forward) imports nothing and keeps the whole universe writing ordered, never-repeating history. When buds sprout two tips (the next and the previous house), nothing ever leaves the now, and under that geometric clock every scrap of "more now" becomes exactly one new house of space. With dice, the now crowds instead and spreads only like √t. The embiggening is the spacetime, in this toy, one for one. A caution learned on the way: a clock that is itself one of the walkers phase-locks onto a single lineage and freezes the rest.
